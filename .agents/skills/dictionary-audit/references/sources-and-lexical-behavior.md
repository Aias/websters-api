Download needed sources once and preserve edition, URL, printed page, scan image identifier, and relevant excerpts. Gutenberg and GCIDE share transcription ancestry. Their agreement can corroborate a representation but does not make a shared error correct. GCIDE also contains later additions and editorial revisions, so the application corpus is not a word-for-word historical facsimile.

For extracted witnesses, label offsets by their encoding and newline representation. Keep native file byte ranges separate from character or UTF-8 ranges computed after newline normalization. Verify that decoding the native range and applying the recorded normalization reproduces the frozen excerpt.

Search grouped headings, aliases, and cross-letter source files before declaring an electronic witness absent. A canonical application key may differ from the source article's other heading. Bind recovered candidates to the complete matching article and retain disagreements instead of inferring ownership from a name match alone.

Before using a corpus comparison to classify absence, verify every source volume and its parsed boundaries. Record the main-alphabet and supplement scopes separately, including omitted files and unparsed headings. Treat a missing comparison match as an unresolved candidate until coverage and lexical identity are established. A matching headword establishes presence only; its text and senses still need reconciliation.

The International print is a related edition, not the exact electronic 1913 source. State the volume and printing accurately when citing it. Use print to resolve identifiable corruption without replacing legitimate electronic-edition differences. Do not add every print-only headword or delete every electronic-only sense. For a substantial omission, seek an electronic witness or surviving source context as well as print.

Use OCR to locate pages, then inspect the actual image for letters, stress, and accents. An archive.org item's `_hocr_pageindex.json.gz` and `_hocr_searchtext.txt.gz` together give the OCR text of each leaf, which `_djvu.txt` does not separate. The image for a zero-based leaf is `https://archive.org/download/<id>/page/n<leaf>.jpg?scale=1`. OCR garbles stress-marked headwords, so locate an entry by each leaf's running heads, the first and last words on the page, rather than by searching for the headword. Printed page numbers, archive leaf numbers, and image URL indices can differ. Confirm the visible page and entry before recording it as evidence. Inspect adjacent entries when a fragment or attribution appears displaced.

## Editorial rulings

The dictionary aims to be correct as of its 1913 publication. Correct a reading when the evidence shows it is wrong, including an error that GCIDE and Gutenberg share and an error in the printed original, as long as the corrected reading would also have been correct in 1913. Leave spelling, taxonomy, pronunciation, and science as they stood then. When print leaves a character ambiguous, choose the character the text means: an etymology that names a Greek letter takes the Greek letter, even where the typeface draws it like a Latin one.

Definitions follow the print to the fullest extent possible. Where GCIDE rewrote a definition or sense after 1913, the 1913 definition replaces the modern wording as a unit, read twice from the print. Apply the same whole-unit restoration to a rewritten printed note or compound definition, including later clauses inside that unit. A separate note absent from the print stays as a later addition. Text and entries that only GCIDE has, such as later senses, WordNet definitions and PJC additions, stay in the record. `reports/dictionary-later-additions.jsonl` classifies each one by source, so it can be filtered or removed later. An entry split across records, such as compounds stranded in a stray key, is merged back into the entry the print places it in.

After source restorations, revalidate later-addition owner pointers from the retained registry. Confirm lexical context before relocating a span, and clear its pointer when the fragment or location remains unresolved. A pointer locates a containing node, which may include printed text outside the classified span. Filtering requires the exact reviewed span and its original-value hash. Leave unresolved rows unchanged until their text and context are settled. The registry's report describes the checks and remaining location questions.

Faithfulness to the print decides structure as well as text. The application's routing, lookup and layout never weigh against a restoration, and a restoration that the application cannot yet show calls for an application change rather than a compromise in the data. A compound the print runs in under its head word belongs in that entry even where the record files it under its own key. Every entry of the 1913 edition gets a record. When an entry has no record and no heading inside another record, a reader transcribes the whole printed entry into a new record in the corpus markup. Senses follow the print's division and numbering, so a batch merges, splits or renumbers record senses to match it, and a sense that only GCIDE has follows the printed senses, numbered on. A missing or partial pronunciation is restored from the print whenever a batch already reviews that entry, and only then. `reports/dictionary-audit-decisions.md` lists the judgment calls made under these rulings.

Evidence still decides each correction. Two certain readings settle what the print shows. A reference current in 1913 can settle which of two readings is right, for example Liddell–Scott–Jones (LSJ) for a Greek word or the Authorized Version for a Bible citation, and the edit records that reference.

## Source recovery decisions

Preserve inherited electronic sense numbers when a later contributor inserted a sense that the conversion excluded. A resulting gap alone does not establish a corrupted numeral. Likewise, a printed heading's different stress pattern does not establish damage when both electronic witnesses consistently preserve the existing heading, unless the existing heading is itself an error, as when it marks two primary stresses where the print and the record's other homographs mark one.

| Finding                                                    | Investigation and correction                                                                                                         |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| U+FFFD, a broken Greek word, or a suspicious phonetic mark | Read a matching witness and the pronunciation guide. Preserve unknown characters when no exact reading is supported.                 |
| A missing article, word, quotation, or definition          | Compare the complete sentence and neighboring source structure. Restore the missing span, not the entire entry from another edition. |
| A numbering gap                                            | Check for an omitted sense, an editorial revision, and genuine numbering before renumbering.                                         |
| An orphan author or quotation                              | Inspect the previous and next headwords. It may reveal missing lexical content or a lost entry boundary.                             |
| An ampersand represented as an alternative                 | Check the source's morphological role. Conjunctive forms and alternative spellings have different meanings.                          |
| A malformed canonical key                                  | Verify the displayed headword and witness, check collisions, and record an explicit rekey with old/new pairing.                      |
| A referenced but absent figure                             | Find the exact diagram or letter arrangement. Preserve its labels and relationship to the definition.                                |

Keep Greek accents, breathing marks, final sigma, ligatures, and reduced-vowel typography faithful to the witness, except where the [editorial rulings](#editorial-rulings) let a 1913 reference settle them. Inspect Unicode code points when appearance is ambiguous. A modern spelling or pronunciation from general knowledge is not a restoration source.

For a letter arrangement or table, preserve the witnessed line breaks and alignment in semantic HTML. For a historical illustration, use the verified source image or an unchanged crop with page URL, crop bounds, dimensions, and useful alternative text. Store shipped images in `public/illustrations/` and document their source there. Do not substitute an invented diagram or remove the definition's figure references to conceal a missing image.

Look for conversion defects even where no replacement character appears: conjunctive grammatical labels rendered as alternatives, lost punctuation after expanded abbreviations, missing reduced-vowel italics or voiced-consonant marks, altered dash types, and normalized ligatures or diaereses. Verify each pattern against source conventions before treating it as corruption. Record corpus-wide patterns for a [dictionary-wide pass](#dictionary-wide-passes) rather than patching them entry by entry. A lone sense number or empty record needs source investigation, not automatic deletion or invented content. GCIDE's source tags show whether a missing sense 2 was a later contributor addition that the conversion filtered out, or 1913 text that was lost.

## Dictionary-wide passes

When a conversion defect recurs across the corpus, fix it once instead of in each batch:

1. Write a pure transform from an entry's key and HTML to corrected HTML. Check each entry against a witness: GCIDE for grammatical labels and pronunciation italics, Gutenberg or print for spellings. Leave an entry unchanged and list it when its witness is missing or disagrees.
2. Dry-run the transform against a frozen copy of the source. Parse every changed entry before and after, and confirm that only the intended characters or markup differ.
3. Apply the passes one at a time through `writeDictionarySource`, and commit each separately so its diff shows one line per changed entry.

Keep the transforms with the evidence. Re-running them after a rebase resolves conflicts. Before patching a recurring pattern in a batch, check `git log -- src/dict.jsonl` for a pass that already covers it. Batch reviewers record further occurrences rather than patching them.

Classify the population before choosing a transform. The same character may encode a stress mark in a heading and an angular measurement in a definition. An abbreviation expansion may lose sentence punctuation while an identical interior expansion is correct. Require witnessed context and negative cases for each rule. Keep source conversion, shared notation normalization, and uncertain readings separate.

## Restoring compounds

GCIDE's editors and our import dropped some 1913 compounds, and the Gutenberg survey (`reports/dictionary-gutenberg-survey.md`) lists them. Before restoring one, search the corpus for its heading, because many already sit under their own key or another entry. A reader writes the restored compound in the record's markup, and both readers place spaces by the corpus majority so that agreement depends only on what the print shows:

- A new block after a line break starts `<br/>  <div class="cs">`.
- A heading the print follows with a comma is `<b>Name</b></div>, `. One followed directly by a label is `<b>Name </b></div><div class="fld">`.
- A label the print follows with a comma is `(Med.)</div>, <div class="cd">`, and one with no comma is `(Med.) </div><div class="cd">`.
- A definition followed by another compound ends `text. </div>&#x2014; <div class="col">`, and the last definition in a block ends `text.</div></div><br/>`.
- A compound that follows a quotation or author starts a new block, with no em dash (1,695 cases against 3).
- A bracketed mark before an em dash is written `[Slang] </div>&#x2014;`.
- A quotation inside a definition is its own `q` block.
- An "as …" example takes the `as` wrapper, with or without a comma.
- A ‖ goes inside `<b>` at the start of a compound heading.
- A binomial is one `spn` element, `<div class="spn">Citrus Japonica</div>` (5,720 cases against 0 split pairs), with a genus the print abbreviates spelled out. A genus after the word "genus" is a `gen` element (1,652 to 0).
- A `part` element keeps its space inside the tag, `face </div>or` (19 to 0).
- A definition followed by an element that renders inline after it, such as an author line, an alternate-spelling bracket or a label, ends with a space before its closing tag: `text. </div><div class="rj">` (13,534 to 1), `text. </div><div class="altsp">` (1,758 to 1), and a sense closes `text.</div></div>` (77,395 to 1,050). A record that ends in a line break ends `<br/> `, with the space (99,483 to 2); 1,281 records end without one, so keep a record's own ending. An author line followed by an em dash ends `Name. </div></div>&#x2014;` (25 to 1).
- A note follows an author line on its own line, `Name.</div></div><br/>  <div class="note">` (188 to 2).
- An apostrophe is `&#x2019;` (5,577 to 3,317 for `&apos;`).
- A pronunciation in a `pr` element writes each mark as a combining hex reference after its base letter, `(se&#x306;r&#x2032;i&#x306;n <i class="it">or</i> -e&#x304;n)` (37,813 combining references to 198 precomposed).
- Restored Greek goes in a `grk` element as hex references (8,124 to 4,597 raw), with the space inside the closing tag before the next word, `Gr. <div class="grk">&#x3B2;&#x3BB;&#x3B1;&#x3C3;&#x3C4;&#x3CC;&#x3C2; </div>sprout` (6,050 to 2,439).
- A cross-reference is an `er` element. Punctuation comes from the print, and every character of `old` stays unchanged, which takes precedence over these rules.

GCIDE's editors often rewrote a whole clause, and the survey splits a rewrite into separate word hunks. Restore a rewording only when the rest of its sentence already matches the print, or widen the span to cover the whole rewritten clause. Replacing one hunk alone can leave a sentence that matches neither text, as with Feast's "when they occurs". The rule covers a one-word spelling change too: Amadou's printed "which grows" agrees with the print's "fungus", so it cannot follow our "fungi".

When a reader rewrites a whole definition to the print, both readers keep `old`'s spaces around the tags that stay, such as the trailing space in `nightmare. </div>` or in `<div class="altname">stellerine </div>`. The trailing space also stays when the reader drops an element the print lacks, so `habitually; <div class="as">…</div>. </div>` becomes `habitually. </div>`, because a following quotation or author renders against the definition without it ("habitually.Spectator."). The corpus splits about 69,000 to 48,000 on that space, so the readers take it from `old` rather than from a corpus majority.

Before rewriting a definition, check whether the record splits the printed sense across several elements, as in `lights a lamp; </div><div class="specif">esp., </div><div class="def">a person who…`. The reader then writes only the part of the print that this element covers, since rewriting it to the whole printed definition repeats the opening on the page. Two names joined by "or" in a compound heading sit in one `mcol`. After a comma the "or" is bare text, `</b></div>, or <div class="col">`, and without one it is `<b>Louver boards </b></div><i class="it">or </i><div class="col">`. Before a label the `mcol` closes `</b> </div></div><div class="fld">`.

## Restructuring senses

A sense-structure batch rewrites a homograph's run of senses to the print. Readers keep the record's markup around printed words and use these corpus forms where they write new markup:

- An example that ends a definition keeps the record's own period placement, because both forms are common (11,564 inside the `as` element, 12,615 outside). A new one closes `…</div>.</div><br/>` at the end of a sense (12,615 to 46) and `…</div>. </div>` before an inline element such as an author line (1,428 to 215) or a quotation (798 to 196).
- A definition before a subsense letter ends `text. </div><div class="sd">` (5,236 to 4).
- A record sense that gives a fuller or modern definition of a printed sense is that sense, even where the print only cross-refers, as with Safety's football sense against the print's "Same as Safety touchdown". A record's examples, clauses and quotations inside a printed sense go with it, as with Torpedo's Farragut quotation.
- A pronunciation target on a homograph checks the owner's own printed heading. Opposite's noun heading has no pronunciation, and the one above it belongs to the adjective.

## Restoring missing entries

An entry the print sets that no record holds, either as its own key or as a heading inside another record, gets a new record. Two readers transcribe the whole printed entry blind and independently, `z10/entry-build.py` accepts only an exact, certain agreement, and `z10/integrate-entries.py` inserts the record in key order. The key is the printed headword without notation, with a ligature spelled out (Haemodynamics) and the print's spelling where Gutenberg has a typo. The readers follow a model record of the same shape and these corpus forms:

- The space after a headword goes inside `</h2>` when a pronunciation follows (22,929 to 2,955), and the part of speech is spelled out.
- A long oo is `oo&#x35E;`, as in Boot (`boo&#x35E;t`), and a ticked macron is `&#x304;&#x30D;`, told from a plain macron or a dot by a pixel dump against a known mark on the same page.
- A comma after an etymon sits outside the `ets` element (16,454 to 2), and where the print italicizes only the formative letters of a word, only those letters are in `ets` (410 cases such as "[Acetic + …]").
- A quotation's italic word is `qex` (35,044 to 12), and a bracketed label keeps its inner space, `[Obs.] </div>` (13,947 to 0).
- "See X, n." is `See <div class="er">X</div>, <div class="pos">noun</div>` (2,035 to 4).
- "[Written also X.]" is `<div class="altsp">[Written also <div class="asp">X</div>.]</div>`, with no inner space (1,253 to 746).
- "n. pl.; sing. X." is `plural noun</div>; <div class="sing"><i class="it">sing. </i><div class="singw">X</div>. </div>` (23 of 24).
- A second part of speech after a dash is `&#x2014; <div class="def2"><div class="pos">noun </div><div class="def">…</div></div>`, as in every corpus case.
- A hyphen at a line end inside a pronunciation is a line break, as in Generatrix's plural "(-trī′-" / "sēz)", so the record keeps `(-tri&#x304;&#x2032;se&#x304;z)`.
- A bracket or other closing mark that the print drops at a line end, and Gutenberg prints, is a printer's error and is restored, as in Globard's "[OE. globerde, from glow.]".
- A printed glyph that cannot belong to the word is kept as printed and held, when no witness shows the intended mark. Estacade's first vowel is a u under a ticked macron in the 1890, 1898 and 1913 printings.
- A pair of headwords the print braces opens the record `<br/> <div class="mhw">{ <h2 …>A </h2>…, <h2 …>B </h2>…</div>, } </div><div class="pos">` (4,081 to 29 for the opening `<br/> `, 3,555 to 556 for the brace kept).
- "called X" with X italic is `called <div class="altname">X</div>` (752 to 636 `xex`, none plain italic), and an italic "or" inside a pronunciation is `<i class="it">or </i>` (1,342 to 649).
- Where readers disagree on whether a printed stress mark is primary or secondary, Gutenberg's headword notation decides it: `"` is the primary mark `&#x2032;` and a backtick the secondary `&#x2CA;`, as Generator (`Gen"er*a`tor`, Gen′er-aˊtor) and Mandarin confirm.

## Lexical structure and boundaries

Preserve source order and association across homographs, header fragments, forms, senses, compounds, notes, usage, and quotations. A repeated-looking passage may carry a distinct lexical function. Remove a duplicate only after proving it is an empty transcription error.

Header parts can interleave a headword, pronunciation, inflection, qualifier, and another headword. Keep punctuation and language qualifiers attached to their original parts. Connectors within a multiple-headword group and grammatical references such as a comparative's "of" phrase remain on that header. Compound introductions belong with their compound rather than the previous numbered sense. Distinguish final run-in derivatives from forms within a continuing numbered sense. Closing every sense at `wordforms` can split the list or detach its quotation. Secondary parts of speech and derivatives can contain definitions and must not collapse into an inflection string.

When a grouped heading shares words across alternatives, preserve the printed ellipsis and record the complete witnessed lexical identities. Check aliases for unintended fragments and newly resolved ambiguities. Keep valid canonical destinations when an alternate spelling collides with another entry.

An attribution between the headword and part of speech belongs to the header, even when punctuation intervenes. Preserve the attribution while retaining the grammatical label in lookup metadata. A body attribution remains with its own passage.

Use `data-scope="homograph"` on a note or synonym list only after verifying whole-word scope. Do not infer that every final note belongs outside the last sense. Preserve nested Roman, numeric, and lettered markers and their content. Preserve witnessed block order when correcting ownership rather than moving a note or quotation around a sense to accommodate the parser. GCIDE overloads `sd` for definitions and references. Use `data-role="definition"` on a witnessed nested definition marker when its role is otherwise ambiguous, and keep unmarked references inline. Do not infer either marker role or following note/author scope from a closing definition wrapper.

Keep a structural issue queue with the affected raw span, parsed owner path, expected witnessed ownership, and a minimal reproduction. A text-only validator's refusal of a tag or scope change routes the issue to the integrator. Prefer the existing explicit role, scope, and headword metadata where markup is ambiguous, with evidence for its value, instead of broadening a heuristic for every superficially similar entry.

Boundary normalization must preserve both spaces and intentional adjacency. For example, joining `frost` and `or rain` needs a word boundary, while joining `some` and `thing` may continue one word. Inspect the source wrappers and punctuation before generalizing. Keep decimals, spaced ellipses, preformatted text, and table spacing intact. Consume compound-separator dashes only at confirmed structural boundaries, retaining adjacent author punctuation and lexical dashes.

The foreign-word mark `‖` belongs with the relevant displayed headword or compound. Exclude it from lookup metadata without dropping or moving it. Parenthetical plural-use labels such as `(pl.)` can occur inside a definition or compound without starting a forms section.

Keep native block markup valid. A quotation inside retained note, usage, or definition HTML still needs quotation presentation and its own attribution. A following author can close that block or its final quotation rather than attaching to an earlier standalone quotation. Tables, preformatted arrangements, and blockquotes cannot be wrapped in an inline element merely because they pass through an inline-HTML component. Preserve malformed table text without assigning it to guessed columns.

## Pronunciation and typography

Consult the tag definitions before changing a class. Many lexical classes imply italics, including taxonomic, etymological, alternate-name, reference, and usage-example classes. Other markers identify metadata rather than visible lexical content. `.ver`, for example, contains technical verification information. Establish the source role before removing a short uppercase token or an encoded sense pointer. A token's appearance alone does not prove it is editorial metadata.

Both audits share a restoration rule for pronunciations:

- A composite placeholder such as `(?; 277)` or `(# or #; 135)` is a damaged field, and so is a field GCIDE marks unreadable with `&unr_;`. Restore it from print when legible and keep the guide number, as in `(zīm; 277)`. When the conversion dropped such a field, insert it after its form word.
- A bare `(?)` or `(#)`, or a pronunciation absent from both the source and GCIDE, stays empty. Do not add pronunciations from print.
- Repair damaged marks inside an existing field when print corroborates them.

Before integration, inventory newly inserted pronunciation fields against the frozen electronic record. Check each headword, homograph, inflection, and derivative separately. An aggregate count of explicit unreadable fields catches unsupported insertions but does not prove correct placement. A complete printed pronunciation with no corresponding electronic field remains ineligible.

Keep cumulative field accounting bound to the original frozen raw value across successive integrations. An earlier restoration does not replenish that owner's insertion allowance. Nested alternate spellings, references, and plurals can own their own fields. A plural's damaged field does not authorize insertion after the singular's bare `(?)`. Verify the exact child owner and ordinal before promoting an inventory match.

Treat eligibility inventories as locators, not ownership judgments. A folded-name match can conflate separate lexical entries or attach a singular form's field to its plural headword. An unreadable Greek etymon does not authorize a pronunciation. Mixed historical and contributor tags require field-level provenance: neither reject an inherited field solely because a paragraph also has a later tag nor accept every unreadable marker in that paragraph.

Preserve witness markup semantics when building inventories. An HTML parser can discard XML-like lexical tags that collide with HTML elements. Verify extracted owners, their order, and original byte or character spans against the witness. Recognize singular, comparative, and superlative form owners. A pronunciation after an `.adjf` belongs to that form, not the preceding headword. Restrict unwrapped damaged pronunciation markers to the corresponding header or form field, not later etymology or definition text.

The exporter can leave a damaged inflection pronunciation outside its `.pr` wrapper. Count it only with an exact frozen fragment and matching unreadable electronic field, then verify the printed form independently. Keep that evidence distinct from tagged-field counts and preserve the original eligibility inventory when adding a correction.

Insertion eligibility does not decide whether an existing phonetic field is correct. Repair a demonstrated copied field or missing character against its own matching witnesses, preserving coherent electronic conventions. An existing unwrapped pronunciation can receive semantic markup and a transcription-token correction without inventing phonetic text. Bind such a wrapper allowance to the exact owner and frozen old/new fragments, separately from unreadable-field eligibility. Never infer a diacritic from a neighboring word.

An exact existing-field transfer is not a new phonetic recovery. For a witnessed lexical-boundary repair, guard both donor and recipient, prove that the complete transferred body and its pronunciation bytes occur once at the destination and are removed from the donor, and verify content conservation across the pair. Keep detached sense or note fields separate from lexical owners rather than inventing owner identities for them.

Pronunciation fields made entirely of unknown placeholders or unknown alternatives are omitted from display while retained in raw source. Composite fields may also contain a pronunciation-guide number, language qualifier, or real phonetic text. The parser preserves a guide reference such as `(#; 73)` as `(73)` while omitting the unknown phonetics. Preserve all meaningful commentary and document the missing transcription. Do not drop an entire field because part of it is unknown.

Include this guide-reference behavior, bare-field eligibility, related-edition limitations, and shared Greek notation conversions in reviewer calibration. Reviewers consult the bound rules before reporting these settled behaviors as defects. A different editorial choice requires an explicit rule decision shared with every active reviewer.

Retain italic reduced-vowel notation within pronunciation HTML. For a double breve or macron spanning two vowels, encode the combining mark after the first base character. Fix a misplaced mark at the shared typography boundary when the source pattern is systematic, then verify text and glyph placement across every affected record.

Read the printed guide and enlarge ambiguous glyphs before transcribing pronunciations. Supply the guide section that illustrates the uncertain mark, not only the stress-mark section. Preserve primary stress `′` (U+2032) versus secondary stress `ˊ` (U+02CA), uptacks such as `ō̍` and `ē̍`, breves on `y`, and italic reduced vowels. A plain macron or a second primary prime is not an equivalent substitute. A related edition's different but valid spelling or phonetic convention alone does not establish conversion damage. The same applies to coherent wording and to labels and punctuation outside the [editorial rulings](#editorial-rulings): a related print’s optional adjective or example is not evidence of a lost word in an intact electronic definition. Require a corroborating eligible witness or a demonstrated corruption, such as an internally conflicting stress mark. For underdotted u, consult the printed guide’s one-dot and two-dot distinctions rather than treating the marks as interchangeable.

Within `.grk`, shared normalization handles word-final sigma and the transcription convention representing an iota subscript as a comma after α, η, or ω. The comma conversion applies before another Greek letter or at the end of a text node, including a standalone `α,`. These are constrained notation conversions, not recovery of missing Greek letters. Keep the conversion scoped to Greek markup and test punctuation and combining-mark boundaries.

The tagset defines `.chform` digits as subscripts. Process its direct text without nesting existing `sub` or changing `sup`, and preserve `.chformi`'s explicit notation. Verify idempotence and unchanged semantic text. Ordinary numbers outside formula markup must remain ordinary numbers.

Use existing application treatments for lexical italics, quotation attribution, and other presentation. Do not change fonts, colors, or design tokens to compensate for a damaged character or a failed font load in the review harness. Verify the computed style before claiming a typography defect.

## Lookup and reference preservation

Check actual reference targets, not just the visible label or a guessed canonical key. Source links can name compounds, alternate spellings, plurals, or an infinitive. Read `entry-aliases.ts`, `lookup.ts`, `slug.ts`, and `db.server.ts` to reproduce current behavior.

When punctuation sits inside a source reference, preserve its visible text. Remove a terminal period from its target only after proving that the dotted URL is unresolved and the undotted URL resolves. A genuine dotted name takes precedence. Build the resolver once from the exact source under review and pass it to preprocessing and parsing, including comparison exporters. Compare identical source with and without the resolver and verify every existing canonical and alias destination. Normalized names alone do not reproduce URL lookup when slug generation drops punctuation.

Canonical slugs resolve before alias fallback. Existing exact aliases and canonical names take precedence over inferred ligature equivalents. A blanket `æ -> ae` or `œ -> oe` lookup normalization can merge distinct existing destinations. Add only unambiguous equivalent spellings and compare every old alias destination against the new map on identical decoded input. A compound defined only by "See under X" makes a deferred claim on its name, so the name goes to an entry that defines the compound and stays with the pointer only when no entry does. A correction that gives a pointer compound the same name as its target therefore needs no hold.

A reference that names a sense, part of speech, or compound links to it through a fragment, following the reference rules in `scripts/PARSING.md`. Follow each such reference and confirm it lands where the print points. When it lands on the entry top, the wrong homograph, or the wrong compound, record the intended id from the target's rendered page as `data-target` on the `.er`, for example `<div class="er" data-target="2-5">Advance</div>, 5.` A recorded target is editorial metadata like `data-scope`. The visible text stays as printed, and a target the print does not name stays unrecorded. The build ignores an id the target entry lacks, so confirm the link after integration. Sense and homograph ids follow their position, so renumbering senses or restoring a homograph requires updating every `data-target` on references to that entry.

If only part of a visible phrase names an entry, the source link boundary may be wrong. Verify the target entry's treatment of the full phrase before changing that boundary. Preserve the visible wording. Do not globally strip particles or words to make broken links resolve.

After a rekey, confirm the old/new record pairing, expected URL, aliases, search, and real navigation. Changes to routing or database generation require checking cross-reference destinations across the affected corpus, including previously valid targets.
