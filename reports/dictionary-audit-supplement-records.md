# Supplement records

This batch reviews the 180 entries of the 1913 Supplement index that match no key, and the nine X through Z entries of the main text that the full-alphabet survey newly exposed. It adds four supplement entries that had no record, Agrotechny, Fuselage, Futurism and Movie, and Cockpit's supplement entry, which the Cockpit record lacked. It opens 19 holds: 13 missing supplement entries in the pool and four outside it that lack an eligible print witness, and two printed entries filed inside another record. The batch grants no complete-entry review credit.

The [ledger](dictionary-audit-supplement-records.jsonl) gives each new record's text, witness and readings, and a disposition for each of the 189 inventory rows.

## Supplement entries under an existing key

The index counts a supplement entry present whenever its headword matches a key, so a supplement entry the key's record lacks goes unseen. Boxer holds its two main-vocabulary nouns but not the supplement's Chinese organization. This batch checked the key-matched supplement entries that have a reachable witness: those on the 23 downloaded pages of the 1907 printing and those in the New International's Addenda of new words. Five have no text in the record:

- **Cockpit** ("In some aëroplanes and flying machines, an inclosure for the pilot or a passenger.") is restored. It follows the printed homograph, as Bowler's derby hat does, under the decision on later homographs.
- **Aerostation** and **Boxer** are held because no printing at hand sets their wording.
- **Telemeter** is held because the 1907 printing sets its words as an added sense, "[Add.] 2.", of the main entry.
- **Trustee process** is held for a decision. The decision on compounds in their own records removed a Trustee process record because Gutenberg prints no separate entry, but the 1913 Supplement does print one.

The other key-matched supplement entries still need the same comparison. A rough text scan flags about 80 whose printed wording no single record holds, many of them later rewordings rather than absences.

## Witnesses

A supplement entry with no record takes the 1900 Supplement in the 1907 Merriam printing as its print witness, and the New International only where the 1907 printing lacks the entry, accepted where it matches Gutenberg word for word. HathiTrust answers every scripted request with a challenge, so only the 23 pages downloaded earlier through the browser show the 1907 printing.

- **Agrotechny:** page 9 of the 1907 printing runs Agrestial, Agrostemma, Agrypnia, Agua. The 1915 New International (leaf 149) and the 1914 London printing (leaf 137) print the entry, and their etymology gives the Greek both electronic texts lost: "[Gr. ἀγρός field, land + τέχνη an art.]". LSJ attests both words with those marks.
- **Fuselage, Futurism and Movie:** the New International sets them in its Addenda of new words, copyright 1913 (1915 leaves 101 and 102, 1914 London leaves 89 and 90). Its 1909 vocabulary took in the earlier supplement, so a word first added in 1913 is absent from the 1907 printing.
- **Readings:** two blind Opus readings of each 1915 strip and one of each 1914 strip agree on every word. Each matches Gutenberg word for word apart from the New International's house style: lowercase headwords, field labels without parentheses, "so-called", "postimpressionism", and an added "[F.]" after Fuselage. The records follow Gutenberg in each.
- **Cockpit:** the New International's Addenda (1915 leaf 100, 1914 London leaf 88) print it, and three blind readings match Gutenberg word for word.
- **Pronunciations:** Movie takes (mo͞ov′ĭ), which all three readers saw. The others are left out, because the readers split on a mark or the New International's ȯ and ė have no counterpart in the record's notation.

For the six whose 1907 page is unreachable, two blind readings of the 1915 New International and, except for Luffa, one of the 1914 London printing are on file. A ruling that admits the New International where the 1907 printing cannot be reached would let the first four apply without new readings. The two filed entries need headwords moved, which the pronunciation pass owns until it finishes.
