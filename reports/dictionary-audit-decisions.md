# Dictionary audit decisions

The user's rulings of 2026-10-28 left the following calls to the auditor. Each entry names the ruling it applies, the decision, and the keys it affects, so a later review can revisit one without re-deriving it. The rulings themselves are in the dictionary-audit skill's sources reference, under "Editorial rulings".

## Rulings applied

- **Faithfulness to the print decides structure.** The application's routing, lookup and layout never weigh against a restoration. Where faithful structure needs application work, the application changes.
- **Pronunciations** are restored from the print only inside entries a batch already reviews.
- **Every 1913 entry gets a record.**
- **Senses follow the print's division and numbering.**

### Compounds in their own records

188 keys, such as Cabbage butterfly, Humming bird and Sodium bicarbonate, hold a compound that the print runs in under its head word. Each merges into its head entry in the printed position, read from the page. These keys were left alone earlier because of how our records are organized, a reason the faithfulness ruling removes.

The merge goes in two steps. Where the head entry already prints the compound and the own record adds no word to it, the own record is a duplicate and is removed (16 records, such as Anchor watch, Blueprint and Trustee process, none of which Gutenberg prints as a separate entry). Where the own record carries text the print lacks, the head entry receives the printed compound and the own record stays as a later-addition entry, because the rulings keep text only GCIDE has; 109 of these 122 records are classified in the later-additions registry, and the other 13 consist mostly of printed text. A pilot of 10 compounds missing from their head entries is read twice blind before its own records are removed.

### Notes and repeated senses in a rewritten run

A note the print lacks entirely moves after the printed senses, so it no longer interrupts them, as Fermentation's industrial-microbiology note does. A record sense whose words the print already sets inside one of its senses is dropped rather than kept as a later addition, as with Canteen's "The sutler's shop in a garrison."

### Reworded senses

A record sense that rewords a printed sense, such as Galaxy's "splendid or impressive" sense against the print's "A splendid assemblage of persons or things", counts as that printed sense, so the printed wording replaces it rather than standing beside it as a later addition. Only a sense whose meaning the print lacks entirely is kept after the printed senses.

### Which pronunciations a batch restores

A batch restores the headword's own pronunciation when the print has one and the record's is missing or partial. Pronunciations inside inflection lists ("Occurred", "Smacked") and plural placeholders such as `(#)` stay for a later pass over those lists, because each needs its own target and crop.

### Later senses after printed ones

When the print divides or numbers senses differently, the record follows the print. A sense that only GCIDE has follows the printed senses and takes the next number. The later-additions registry keeps classifying it.

### Stress where entry readers split

Where two readers of a new entry differ on a headword stress mark, Gutenberg's headword notation decides it. Generatrix takes `Genˊer-a′trix` from Gutenberg's ``Gen`er*a"trix``, and Hiphalt takes a secondary final mark. A stress mark that both readers agree on stands even where Gutenberg differs, as with Gunsmith and Jailer.
