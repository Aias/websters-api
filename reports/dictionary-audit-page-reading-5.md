# Page-reading batch 5

Two models read printed pages 251 to 350 without seeing the record, and their agreed reading is compared with the record word by word. This batch makes 646 corrections in 516 entries, adds 6 entries that the electronic text never had, and gives 7 records their printed spelling. It opens 18 holds: 13 questions in the review queue and 5 entries whose homographs stand in another order than the print's. The batch grants no complete-entry review credit.

The method is the one [batch 2](dictionary-audit-page-reading-2.md) describes, with the changes of batches [3](dictionary-audit-page-reading-3.md) and [4](dictionary-audit-page-reading-4.md). This report gives what differs.

## Method changes

- **Pages:** leaves n356 to n455 of the 1907 volume 1 scan, Choice through Cruelty, about 244,000 words in 6,155 printed entries.
- **Readers:** Opus is the first reader and Sonnet the second, for every column.
- **Tiebreak reading:** Sonnet misreads more often than Opus, so the pair misses a difference wherever Sonnet misreads the word. The first reader alone is therefore also compared with the record. It found 33 differences the pair had missed. Fable read a strip of the same scan for each one, blind, and agreed with Opus on 22. Those 22 join the other differences and are then read on the 1898 printing like the rest. Nine of them became corrections, such as Cliented "pettifoggers" for "pettifiggers" and Crouch "crucchen" for "cruchen".
- **Second chance on the 1898 printing:** a strip that did not settle a difference is cut again, twelve lines tall, from the place the first reader found the line. Of 24 such strips, 14 then read as the print and 1 as the record.
- **Inflected forms:** differences inside the bracket of inflected forms are no longer set aside. Most are the record's written-out labels. Twelve were misspelled forms, such as Covet "Coveted" for "Covered" and Convolvulus "Convolvuluses" for "Convoluluses".
- **Proofreading:** three reviewers read all 501 rule-drafted corrections for sense before any was applied, and reported 24. Thirteen would have blended a sentence that GCIDE rewrote, six left a doubled space, and the rest carried over a misprint or a label.
- **Blended passages:** before applying, every passage where some differences are corrected and others are left is listed and read whole.

## Corrections

- **617 follow the ruling of 10 November 2026.** The record's reading came from the typists. Examples: Contrary "froward" for "forward", Crisp "sparkling" for "sparking", Condition "rescission" for "recission", Countercheck's authority "Newman" for "Newton", Collateral's "Atterbury" for "Attebury", Coffin's citation "Gen. l. 26" for "Gen. 1. 26", and plain typing slips such as "appelation", "allevation", "particulary" and "nesessity". Confederacy regains "covenant", Commerce regains "souls of the", and Constitution regains "a nation, state, society, or other organized body of".
- **29 undo a later GCIDE change.** Examples: Christendom and Comboloio "Mohammedan" for "Muslim", Constantia "Cape Colony" for "South Africa", Coracle "Thibet", Clutch "gripe" for "grip", Cortege "procession", Crow's nest "perch" for "platform", and Crucifer "order" for "family". Coach regains "a trainer; esp. one who trains a boat's crew for a race".
- **Whole lines return:** Classical regains sense 4, "(Eccl.) Of or pertaining to a classis." Cocket regains sense 4, "The second quality of bread." Conversation regains the quotation "Be ye holy in all manner of conversation. 1 Pet. i. 15." Copy regains "The copy is at the press. Dryden." Coo and Cool regain their etymologies.
- **Cockle** had the transitive and intransitive verbs run together. They are two entries again, as printed.
- **Concerning, Conjecturally, Conscription and Contrite** each carried a neighbouring printed entry under their own headword. Those entries are now the records Concernment, Conjecturality, Conscriptional and Contritely, and Concerning regains its own noun sense, "Concern; business. [Obs.] Shak."
- **Contorniate** gives its second headword as the print does, "Contorniato".

Usage marks gain what the print sets in 9 entries, and 5 definitions regain the authority printed after them.

Four doubled spaces that batch 4 left where it removed a word are closed (Bottom, Bougie, Cat's-paw and Charge). The rule that drafts a removal now leaves one space.

## Kept and held

- **The print is wrong:** Chromatophore doubles a letter where "numerous" breaks across two lines, and Chrysene misspells "naphthalene".
- **A convention keeps the record:** Croesus writes "Gr." where the print sets "G." before a Greek word. Circle spells out the abbreviated heading "Voltaic circle or circuit". Cooee follows the 1913 Supplement.
- **The 1898 printing reads as the record (24):** worn or broken type in the 1907 impression, such as Christendom "dcctrine" and Condemn "floeks".
- **Held for the person (13 questions):** twelve passages that GCIDE rewrote, where restoring the printed wording removes text the later-additions register lists or would blend two wordings (Circumstantiate, Cockateel, Code, Collectivistic, Conglomeration, Conjugate, Contagious, Continental, Contributor, Copyright, Coup and Croat). The thirteenth is Cliency, which both printings set as "Cliensy". Each is in the review queue with crops of both printings.
- **Held for a later pass (5):** Circumstance, Circumstantial, Cow, Crock and Crook hold their homographs in another order than the print's.

## Limits

- Pronunciation marks, punctuation, capitals and italics are not compared.
- Where the tiebreak reading disagreed with the first reader or was unclear (11 places), no difference is raised.
- An entry that runs over from page 250 or onto page 351 is compared only as far as this batch's pages.
- The 1913 printing itself was not read. The only scan found is access-restricted.
