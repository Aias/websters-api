# Page-reading batch 18

Two models read printed pages 1561 to 1681 without seeing the record, and their agreed reading is compared with the record word by word. This batch makes 609 corrections in 463 entries, adds 9 entries that had no record of their own, and gives 10 records their printed spelling. It opens 17 holds: 14 questions in the review queue and three differences in the order of same-spelled entries. The batch grants no complete-entry review credit.

These are the last pages of the main vocabulary. With this batch the page reading covers every printed page from 1 to 1681.

The method is the one [batch 2](dictionary-audit-page-reading-2.md) describes, with the changes of batches [3](dictionary-audit-page-reading-3.md) to [12](dictionary-audit-page-reading-12.md). One thing was added for this batch: the word lists under Un- are compared as sets, as described below.

## Pages and readers

- **Pages:** leaves n608 to n728 of the 1895 volume 2 scan, Ultime through Zythum, about 299,000 words in 7,809 printed entries.
- **Readers:** Opus and Sonnet. Opus alone found 78 differences the pair had missed, Fable read those strips blind and agreed on 49, and 13 became corrections. A second, taller strip of the 1898 printing settled 28 of 50 differences that the first strip had left unclear.
- **Proofreading:** three reviewers read all 415 rule-drafted corrections and reported 55. Thirteen of the reports are single words where the 1913 typists' text agrees with the record and both readings make sense. Under the ruling of 10 November 2026 those follow the print, and the ledger lists them. Reports that a correction would mix the printed wording with a later rewrite went to the person.

## The word lists under Un-

The entry Un- holds three long lists of words formed with the prefix, 2,088 in all. The print sets them three across, so a line of the reading takes one word from each of three runs of the alphabet, and the line-by-line comparison cannot follow them. It raised 687 false differences there, which the ledger marks as such.

The lists were compared as sets instead. Every list word that both readers have was checked against the record's list, and the reverse. The record had ten words misspelled and lacked one, and both readers and the 1898 printing agree on all eleven.

## Corrections

- **576 follow the ruling of 10 November 2026.** The record's reading came from the typists. Examples: Upon "assure" for "assume", Universal "benefice" for "beneficence", Usurper "not" for "nor", Way "process" for "progress", and the field labels "(Anat.)" for "(Astron.)" under Venus and "[Obs.]" for "(Zoöl.)" under Wentletrap. Unison regains its I. Taylor quotation, Witch a quotation from Beaumont and Fletcher, Wish a line of Young, Wing "(b) On the road; moving from one place to another", and Usurpation "place, power, functions, or the like, without right". Wine regains two stretches of its etymology.
- **33 undo a later GCIDE change.** Viscosity sense 2 again reads "A quality analogous to that of a viscous fluid", Under regains the phrase "Under the rose", Vandalism "their monuments", and Underbid "less" for "lower". Viceroy and Water tree regain the printed form of a name with two genera, and Vanadium its printed atomic weight.
- **Inflected forms:** 10 are corrected, among them Universality "Universalities", Violate "Violated", Wad "Wadded" and Warrantable "Warrantably".
- **Undershrieve** had the definition of the next printed entry, the noun Undershrub. It again reads "An undersheriff. [Obs.]", and Undershrub has its noun before its adjective.
- **Undiscreet and Weanling** each carried a neighbouring printed entry under their own headword. Undiscretion and Weaning are now records of their own.
- **Small slips fixed by hand:** Ungored regains its authority "Shak.", Watch "to watch a rogue", and Unruly loses a repeated example.
- **Three corrections lie outside these pages.** Proofreaders of the punctuation pass noticed them beside the marks they were checking, and both readers of those pages agree on each. Mistletoe, on page 931, read "is akin of" for "is akin to". Greek, on page 649, had a question mark where the print has the word "Γραικός". Hemitone, on page 685, had taken its cross-reference for its definition, and again reads "See Semitone.] Same as Semitone."

## Kept and held

- **The print is wrong (13):** broken letters give "tc" under Unquestioned, "cl." under View, "fcr" under Ward, "ircn" under Wolframite, "sc" under Wreathe and "Hindoc" under Yuga. Milton is misquoted with "desight" under Vagancy and "fruithful" under Vigor. Witch has "Aynesi" for Agnesi and Wretched "Wailer" for Waller. Visionary, Wedge-tailed and Wish each have a damaged word.
- **Probable 1913 revisions:** Ultraviolet, Umlaut, Uredo, Uredospore, Voided, Volt, Volume, Vulcanite, Washer, Watt, Wekau, Ye, Yellow and Yen stand as the 1913 typists' text has them. Wekau is the "New Zealand owl" that the older printings call Australian.
- **Held for the person (14 questions):** passages that GCIDE rewrote, where restoring the printed wording removes later text or would blend two wordings: Unbelieving, Unconscionable (two), Uncypher, Underlying, Viable, Virus, Visceral, Visé, Visualize, Wire-puller, Wreak, Yield and Zebra. Each is in the review queue with crops of both printings.
- **Held for a later pass (3):** Wager, Welt and Yare, whose same-spelled entries stand in a different order from the print.

The later-additions register lists only whole entries under X, Y and Z, not phrases. Every edit to an entry under those letters was therefore checked by hand, and the two rewritten passages found there, Yield and Zebra, are among the questions.

That is about 391,000 tokens a page and 6,100 an entry. The integrating session's own usage was not measured. About 3.3 million of the third-reading tokens went on the false differences in the Un- lists.

## Limits

- Pronunciation marks, punctuation, capitals and italics are not compared here. The [punctuation and capitals pass](dictionary-audit-punctuation.md) covers two of those.
- A single word that the 1913 printing changed cannot be told from a typist's slip without the 1913 page. The ruling of 10 November 2026 treats it as a slip.
- Other lists set in several columns may hide the same kind of difference that the Un- lists did. None was found on these pages.
- The 1913 printing itself was not read. The only scan found is access-restricted.
