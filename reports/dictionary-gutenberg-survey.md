# Survey of our text against Gutenberg's 1913 transcription

Our source descends from GCIDE, which began as the same volunteer transcription of the 1913 _Webster's Revised Unabridged_ that Project Gutenberg distributes (etexts 660–669). GCIDE's editors later reworded definitions, added senses, and fixed transcription typos. Our macOS-bundle import then dropped some of the added material and, with it, some 1913 text that sat next to it. Fabulous shows both kinds of drift: sense 2 reads "Beyond belief" where the print and Gutenberg read "Passing belief", and the compound "Fabulous age" is missing.

The survey compares every entry three ways: our text, Gutenberg's, and the OCR of the scanned print (vol. 1 of the 1907 printing and vol. 2 of the 1895 printing). It normalizes notation, Greek, and markup, diffs the words, and classifies each changed hunk. For each hunk, it scores which reading the OCR supports. The data and tools are in `.context/dictionary-audit/survey-gutenberg/`, which is local to a workspace.

There are also 7,019 keys that only our text has, mostly later GCIDE additions, and 515 Gutenberg entries with no key in ours.

## How far the vote can be trusted

A stratified sample of 60 hunks was checked against the page images.

- **The vote matched the page on 54 of 60.** An "ours" vote whose Gutenberg side scored zero is not evidence, because it usually means the OCR window was garbled.
- **Neither source wins outright.** Where the page decides, it showed Gutenberg on 37 and ours on 21.
- **Gutenberg was right** on most substitutions. Those are editorial rewrites in ours: "Lack" for "Want", "Rumania" for "Roumania", "cannot" for "can not", and senses that were rewritten or added.
- **Ours was right** on most spelling hunks. Those are Gutenberg typos, dropped ligatures, and doubled words.
- **Text only ours has splits evenly.** Half is text Gutenberg dropped, and half was added after 1913.
- **Every sampled compound that ours lacks is in the print.**

A vote alone would make roughly one wrong edit in ten, so every hunk goes through the usual two blind print readings before it changes the source. Two checks hold up well enough to prioritize the work: a "gutenberg" vote, and a compound that ours lacks.

## Results so far

- **Compounds.** Of the 113 compounds the print supports, 63 already sit elsewhere in our corpus, often split into their own entry. Six are parsing artifacts. Of the 44 targets built from the rest, 33 are restored after two blind readings. The other 11 wait on markup conventions or on printed punctuation the readers read differently.
- **Pilot wording.** Seven pilot corrections restore the print's wording, including Fabulous, Vise, Viable, Hospodar and Pathogene.
- **Headwords.** 1,662 lowercase headwords whose 1913 headword is capitalized now take the capital.
- **Short rewordings.** A batch took 48 short substitutions with a strong print vote. Of these, 36 sat in sentences that GCIDE had also rewritten elsewhere, so changing the hunk alone would leave a sentence that matches neither text. Those stay held. The 12 whose sentences otherwise match the print are restored: "want" for "lack", "Mohammedans" for "Muslims", "occluding" for "absorbing", "albumen" for "protein" and similar.
- **Whole definitions.** Three batches replaced definitions that GCIDE rewrote with the printed definition read twice blind. The batches chose senses where one record sense pairs with one printed sense. The first restored 45 of 48. The second restored 56 of 60, and three of the 60 already matched the print. The third restored 44 of 46, and the other two already matched. Smack waits on a ruling because its record merges two printed senses. The final check caught two definitions that repeated text, because the record splits the sense around a `specif` element ("lights a lamp; esp., a person who…"). It also caught three that ran into the following author line. All five are corrected. Targets now skip senses that the record splits, and a script compares each accepted fragment with the text around it.
- **Held targets and defects.** A fix batch reran the 15 compound and definition targets held for conventions, settling each from corpus counts. It also repaired defects the final checks found: Journalist's "Addison.", Take's "quantity of copy", Omnibus's stray "‖", Shed's lost etymology characters with "to part" and "perh.", Locust's "Œdipoda, or Pachytylus", and Backwoodsman's "older portions of". All 19 are restored. A second fix batch repaired 13 more found by later final checks, among them Clock's "all perh.", Metameric's lost Greek μέρος and its reworded note, Passable's "the slander", Serine's printed pronunciation, a duplicated Pea jacket headword and a stray "‖" on Pet. A third restored ten more, and a fourth added Dactylology's "the Abbé", Macadamize's "Macadam" and Jeffersonian's printed definition with its "Lowell." author line. The third's repairs include Amadou's "fungus … which grows", Materiel's "That in a complex system which constitutes", Lama's "Thibet" and two pronunciations whose characters were lost.
- **Spellings.** A pilot of 48 one-word hunks where the print supports Gutenberg restored 45 printed spellings, such as "Thibet", "Hindoo", "Hayti", "bowlders" and "caviare", and typo fixes such as "galley" for "gallery" and "relation" for "relating". The record's spelled-out "Appendix." stays where the print abbreviates. Two were reverted after the final check because GCIDE had also changed the subject the word agrees with: Amadou's "which grows" goes with the print's "fungus", not our "fungi".
- **Missing words.** A pilot of 48 gaps where the print has words ours lacks restored 13. Most gaps sit inside clauses GCIDE rewrote, so filling the gap alone leaves a sentence that matches neither text: Manure would read "dung, … dung" and Flesh "potassium phosphate of potash". The readers held 28 of these, and the final check caught 7 more that were reverted. These went to whole-definition restoration instead. Of the 64 held spans from this pilot and the short-rewording batch, 15 sit in a definition that pairs with one printed sense. A fourth definition batch restored 13 of them, among them Grind, Knapsack, Pacific and Shah, and two wait on a structural pass because the print draws the sense boundary differently.
- **Spacing.** 37 entries ran a definition into the next compound, part of speech or label ("glass.— Manganese bronze"), where the corpus spaces the closing tag 12,899 times to 8 before a dash and 27,033 to 30 before a label. Each is now spaced.
- **Sense structure.** A batch rewrote 19 entries whose senses the record divides differently from the print, so their senses now follow the print's division, order and numbering: Peach and Rennet each take the print's single definition, Round its 17 senses in printed order, Smack its two senses. Later senses the print lacks entirely stay after the printed ones. The same batch restored 13 missing pronunciations in those entries and completed three held merges: Cretacic into Cretaceous, the Atterbury quotation into Hostility, and the printed "Critical point" compound in Critical, which replaces the reworded Critical temperature record. A second batch did the same for 24 more, among them Colony, Galaxy, Mechanism, Obituary and Seduction, and restored their missing pronunciations.
- **Compounds in their own records.** 188 records hold a compound the print runs in under its head word. Sixteen duplicated a compound their head entry already prints and are removed. Two batches inserted 46 printed compounds into their head entries from the page (Halfway house, Noah's ark, Jumping jack, Mandarin duck, Sodium bicarbonate and others). Ten of those records held nothing beyond the printed compound and are removed; the rest also carry text only GCIDE has and stay as later additions.
- **Editorial markers.** GCIDE's WordNet sense markers, such as "[wns=3]" after Pacific's definition, rendered as text in 68 entries, and its homograph indexes, such as "ferment{1}", in 151 more. All are removed, except that Lamp's "See sense {3}" keeps its number as "See sense 3".
- **Split entries.** Eleven entries that sat in the wrong records are merged back where the print places them. A stray "Bold eagle" record held Bold's compounds, and Bark's tree-bark noun sat in Bard. Four more proposals wait on judgment: Cretaceous, Hostility, Dormant and Critical.

## Next steps

- **Text and entries only ours has.** These stay, and `reports/dictionary-later-additions.jsonl` classifies each by source so a build can filter them.
- **Rewritten definitions.** The ruling restores each printed definition whole. The method holds where a record sense pairs with one printed sense, but that pool is nearly used up: after the second batch, 46 of the remaining 427 candidates pass location. Senses that ours splits, merges or renumbers differently from the print need a structural pass first.
- **Pronunciations.** Gutenberg prints "(?)" for 66,411 respelled pronunciations that its transcribers did not encode. GCIDE carries the same gap as 68,805 `<pr>(?)</pr>` placeholders, and our import dropped them, so these entries show no pronunciation where the print has one (Desiccator, Diallyl, Nitromethane). Restoring them means transcribing each pronunciation from the print.
- **Entries only Gutenberg has.** Of the 515 Gutenberg entries with no key in ours, 402 sit in our records under a variant spelling or as a heading inside another record (Ablet in Ablen, Above-mentioned in Above-named), 12 are word forms of an existing entry, and 26 are survey artifacts. 75 are missing, since Aisleless turned out to have a record of its own. A first batch transcribed 24 of them from the page into new records (Callisection, Gunsmith, Handmade, Ketchup, Palola and others), and a second batch added 23 more, among them Dinornis, Euphemism, Generatrix, Koran and Neptunium. The rest follow.
- **Partial pronunciations.** 3,994 pronunciations in 3,714 entries keep only some of their characters or only a placeholder, such as Azure's "(?; 277)". Under the ruling, a batch restores the headword pronunciation of every entry it already reviews; the sense-structure, fix and compound batches have restored 41 so far.
- **Open holds** are in `reports/dictionary-audit-holds.jsonl`, each with the question it waits on.
