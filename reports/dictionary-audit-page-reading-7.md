# Page-reading batch 7

Two models read printed pages 451 to 550 without seeing the record, and their agreed reading is compared with the record word by word. This batch makes 755 corrections in 577 entries, adds 19 entries that the electronic text never had, and gives 10 records their printed spelling. It also restores two passages on earlier pages that the 1913 printing had probably revised. It opens 18 holds: 15 questions in the review queue and 3 entries whose homographs stand in another order than the print's. The batch grants no complete-entry review credit.

The method is the one [batch 2](dictionary-audit-page-reading-2.md) describes, with the changes of batches [3](dictionary-audit-page-reading-3.md) to [6](dictionary-audit-page-reading-6.md). This report gives what differs.

## Method changes

- **Pages:** leaves n556 to n655 of the 1907 volume 1 scan, Dragonnade through Fellowship, about 247,000 words in 6,752 printed entries.
- **Readers:** Opus and Sonnet. Opus alone found 50 differences the pair had missed, Fable read those strips blind and agreed on 31, and 15 became corrections. A second, taller strip of the 1898 printing settled 36 of 45 differences that the first strip had left unclear.
- **Probable 1913 revisions:** the record descends from a transcription typed from the 1913 printing, which Project Gutenberg preserves. Where that transcription and the record agree on a whole phrase and the 1898 and 1907 printings agree on a different one, a typist's slip does not explain it. The 1913 printing itself was probably revised, and the record stays. Etesian is the case here: its definition differs throughout. The same holds for a correct reading in the record against a misprint in the older printings, such as Feather's "ravines" for "ruins" in a quotation from Scott.
- **Earlier batches:** three corrections of this kind are undone. Ablaut again gives the examples "hang, hung" (batch 2 had "man, men"), and Bluff-bowed again reads "Built with the stem nearly straight up and down" (batch 4).
- **Records and edits together:** a drafted rename or rewrite replaces a whole record, so a word-level correction to the same record is folded into the draft before either is applied.

## Corrections

- **686 follow the ruling of 10 November 2026.** The record's reading came from the typists. Examples: Emunctory "émunctoire", Eleven "ānleofan", Dulcimer "dolcemele, or Sp.", Ettin "AS." for "SA.", Farrow without a stray "a", Fantasticality "Fantasticalness" for "Fantastically", and the authorities "Whately", "Thirlwall", "Sayce" and "Burrill". Elephant regains the phrase "Elephant shrew", Fairy its sense 3, Feastful the quotation "Feastful friends", and Dumb, Dungeon, Ducat, Fricassee and Friday parts of their etymologies.
- **69 undo a later GCIDE change.** Druggist again ends "also, a pharmaceutist or apothecary", and its note reads "The same person often carries on the business of the druggist and the apothecary." Epidemiology is again "That branch of science which treats of epidemics", Eucalyptol "A volatile, terpenelike oil extracted from the eucalyptus, and consisting largely of cymene", and Faze "See Feeze." Expansion's commercial sense, Ether's second sense, Entrepreneur and Dummy's sense 5 regain their printed wording.
- **Inflected forms:** 25 are corrected, among them Electioneer "Electioneered", Escheat "Escheated", Eschew "Eschewed, Eschewing", Estop "Estopped", Exhale "Exhaled, Exhaling" and Fat "Fatting". Emporium, Eozoon, Ephor and Epithalamium regain the label "E." before their English plurals.
- **Exploit and Fag** had lost a heading: Exploit's verb and Fag's noun stand again where the print sets them.
- **Elater, Elector, Engender, Exsect, Exuperation, Exuscitate, Fahlband and Fallowness** each carried a neighbouring printed entry. Those entries are now the records Elaterin, Electoral, Engenderer, Exsection, Exurgence, Exustible, Fahlore and Fallway, and the eight read as printed.

## Kept and held

- **Held for the person (15 questions):** the table of elements under Element, which the record has replaced with a modern table. The note under Element that goes with it. Escorial and Escurial, which the record has swapped. Each is in the review queue with crops of both printings.

## Limits

- Pronunciation marks, punctuation, capitals and italics are not compared.
- A single word that the 1913 printing changed cannot be told from a typist's slip without the 1913 page. The ruling of 10 November 2026 treats it as a slip.
- An entry that runs over from page 450 or onto page 551 is compared only as far as this batch's pages.
- The 1913 printing itself was not read. The only scan found is access-restricted.
