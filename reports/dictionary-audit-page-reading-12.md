# Page-reading batch 12

Two models read printed pages 961 to 1060 without seeing the record, and their agreed reading is compared with the record word by word. These are the first pages of volume 2. This batch makes 639 corrections in 513 entries, adds 4 entries that the electronic text never had, and gives 9 records their printed spelling. It opens 66 holds: 61 questions in the review queue and 5 entries whose homographs stand in another order than the print's. The batch grants no complete-entry review credit.

The method is the one [batch 2](dictionary-audit-page-reading-2.md) describes, with the changes of batches [3](dictionary-audit-page-reading-3.md) to [10](dictionary-audit-page-reading-10.md). Two things differ from here on.

- **The first scan is the 1895 printing.** Volume 2 is read from the Internet Archive copy `webstersinternat02websuoft`, where printed page = leaf + 953. The 1898 printing still confirms each difference.
- **Fable drafts, proofreads and writes the questions.** Opus did this through batch 11. Opus and Sonnet still read the pages.

## Pages and readers

- **Pages:** leaves n8 to n107 of the 1895 volume 2 scan, N through Penitentiary, about 245,000 words in 7,458 printed entries.
- **Readers:** Opus and Sonnet. Opus alone found 70 differences the pair had missed, Fable read those strips blind and agreed on 43, and 11 became corrections. A second, taller strip of the 1898 printing settled 22 of 38 differences that the first strip had left unclear.
- **Proofreading:** three reviewers read all 498 rule-drafted corrections and reported 123. Forty-nine of the reports are single words where the 1913 typists' text agrees with the record and both readings make sense. Under the ruling of 10 November 2026 those follow the print, and the ledger lists them. Reports that a correction would mix the printed wording with a later rewrite went to the person.

## Corrections

- **478 follow the ruling of 10 November 2026.** The record's reading came from the typists. Examples: Pass "transition" for "transmission", Pathless "through" for "trough", Outact "widow's" for "window's", Necrolatry "Spencer" for "Spenser", and Nourish "holland". Parachute regains its printed ending, "by means of which a descent may be made from a balloon, or any eminence". Pass regains a lost sense with its line from Shakespeare, and Partridge the phrase "Sea partridge".
- **161 undo a later GCIDE change.** Osmose, Papaw and Orthopedy regain the printed definitions that GCIDE had replaced with "Same as osmosis", "Same as papaya" and "Same as orthopedics". Others: Paramagnetism "Magnetism, as opposed to diamagnetism", Oo "A beautiful bird (Moho nobilis) of the Hawaiian Islands", Nayaur "Nepaul", and the printed atomic weights of Nickel, Osmium, Oxygen and Palladium.
- **Nave** regains its second entry, the nave of a church, which no record carried.
- **Inflected forms:** 13 are corrected, among them Nativity "Nativities", Navvy "Navvies", Nib "Nibbed", Outrage "Outraged" and Paterfamilias "Patresfamilias".
- **Novercal** began with a copy of the entry Novennial under the wrong headword. The copy is removed, and Novennial keeps its own record. Parti-colored gains the printed pair "Party-colored, Parti-colored", which its cross-reference had pointed to in vain.
- **Small slips fixed by hand:** the authorities "Chaucer" under Nay, "Shak." under Nave and "Abp. Bramhall" under Necessitation had lost their first letter, and O read "promoted by surprise" for "prompted by surprise".

## Limits

- Pronunciation marks, punctuation, capitals and italics are not compared.
- A single word that the 1913 printing changed cannot be told from a typist's slip without the 1913 page. The ruling of 10 November 2026 treats it as a slip.
- An entry that runs onto page 1061 is compared only as far as this batch's pages.
- The 1913 printing itself was not read. The only scan found is access-restricted.
