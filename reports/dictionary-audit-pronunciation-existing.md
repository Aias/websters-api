# The record's own pronunciations

About 23,000 headwords had a whole pronunciation before the [pronunciation pass](dictionary-audit-pronunciation.md) began. This pass compares those with the two blind page readings, reads the ones both readers question on the 1898 printing, and corrects 469. The [ledger](dictionary-audit-pronunciation-existing.jsonl) has a row for each pronunciation that was read again.

## Which ones were read again

20,757 entries have a whole pronunciation in the record and one from each reader.

| How the record stands against the two readings       | Entries |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | ------: |
| The record and both readers are the same             |  14,281 |
| The record sides with one reader at every letter     |   2,438 |
| The record has a mark of a kind readers miss         |   1,132 |
| The record spells out what the print abbreviates     |   1,592 |
| Both readers have another mark                       |     304 |
| The record and the two readers give three marks      |     511 |
| Both readers have other letters, hyphens or stresses |     419 |
| One reader has other letters, hyphens or stresses    |      80 |

The last four rows, 1,314 entries, were read again. A mark of a kind readers miss is no evidence against the record, and a pronunciation that the record spells out where the print abbreviates is a question for the review queue.

## Rule

The entry's first lines were cut from the 1898 printing, enlarged three times, and read blind. A letter, a stress or a mark changes only where that reading and both page readings give the same thing. Three more conditions hold a change back. The record keeps a mark of a kind readers miss, such as the tick on a long vowel, even when no reading shows it. A stressed vowel keeps its mark when the readings show none, because a vowel in a stressed syllable is never printed bare and the page has lost the mark. And an entry must be paired with its printed entry for certain by both readers.

Corrections include "(fẽr)" to "(fĭp′p’l)" under Fipple; "(să′chem)" to "(sā′chem)" under Sachem; "(păth′ĭc)" to "(păth′ĭk)" under Pathic; "(ĭm-pĕrˊȧ-tō′rĭ-val)" to "(ĭm-pĕrˊȧ-tō′rĭ-al)" under Imperatorial; "(bȧ-sĭs′ẽr-ōt)" to "(bȧ-sĭs′ẽr-īt)" under Basicerite; "(thẽr′mŏl′ō̍-jy̆)" to "(thẽr-mŏl′ō̍-jy̆)" under Thermology; "(ăl′ma-năk; 277)" to "(a̤l′mȧ-năk; 277)" under Almanac.

## Limits

- Only pronunciations after a headword are covered, and only where both readers pair the entry with a printed one.
- Where the two readers differ from each other and from the record, one closer reading does not settle the mark, and the record stays.
- The corrected pronunciation takes the weight of each accent from the headword, as the pronunciation pass does.
